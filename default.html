Marketing

signals, displays, and processes used to advertise the value of something

by Charles Iliya Krempeaux

There is a line in an old movie that often gets misquoted. The misquoted version of the line is:

If you build it, they will come.

(The actual line from the 1989 movie "Field of Dreams" is: If you build it, he will come. But, that is neither here nor there.)

That misquoted line (“if you build it, they will come”) I think captures the (implicit or explict) mistaken belief that many people have when they first create a product, offer a service, etc — that customers will magically show up and give them money if they just "build it".

If you just build it (and don't do any marketing) then almost certainly no one will come.

What many people, businesses and companies eventually realize is: just because you build (or create or offer) something, does not mean customers will come pay for it.

Marketing is important. It is important for businesses, it is important for start-ups, it is important for individuals. In fact, marketing is important for biological life-forms in general.

I suspect that most people who talk about marketing are thinking about marketing done by businesses. I.e., the process of connecting what a business makes or offers with the people who need it, want it, or are just willing to pay for it.

But, marketing also including marketing done by biological life-forms, includes propaganda, and includes other things.

Definition

Marketing is the deployment of structured signals — whether honest, exaggerated, or deceptive — which are designed to affect and even manipulate the perceptions, choices, or behaviors of independent receivers to benefit the fitness, interests, or survival of the sender.
